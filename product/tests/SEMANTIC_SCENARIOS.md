# Qualitative operating scenarios

These are invented, stipulated cases for evaluating the Product caller/reviewer
behavior. They are not evidence that any real game is good or bad. Python fixtures
only test record/gate mechanics. A fresh semantic reviewer should apply the actual
skill/workflow to each case, give a scoped judgment or uncertainty, explain the
causal reason, and select the smallest useful next move. Do not add hidden player
observations, invent an adopted price, or prescribe extra features by default.

## A. A genuinely good mechanism in a weak purchase

A $14.99 desktop tactics game targets players who enjoy adapting compact builds.
The current approved premise is coherent. In observed play the first three fights
produce interesting adaptations. By fight five, the same dominant build solves
all observed opponents; promised later pressures have not been encountered. Art
and sound convey state clearly, but the shop and transitions feel like test panels,
and the product promises a crafted campaign with changing pressures. All automated
tests pass. The producer argues that Idea approval and a functioning full loop
mean the product is finished. What does Product conclude and do next?

## B. Expensive visual craft surrounding empty interaction

A $29.99 atmospheric exploration title targets buyers seeking an emotionally
expressive interactive journey. The final visuals and audio have been inspected
and are unusually coherent and accomplished. Full play reveals that repeated
interactions do not change anything, the advertised choice has no consequence,
and long walks delay the next identical interaction. The author proposes three
more environments to increase playtime and says the art budget justifies price.
How should Product reason about the whole offer and the next useful change?

## C. Brief, complete and expressive

A $11.99 single-session work is explicitly sold to an audience seeking a compact,
intimate interactive story. Its current store description is honest about a brief
one-sitting experience. Full observed play shows choices expressed through tactile
interaction, an affecting completed arc, integrated restrained art/audio, clear
input and a deliberate ending. It makes no promise of replay, branching abundance
or long-term mastery. The team wants to add procedural side content because the
runtime is short. What should Product judge, and when should production stop?

## D. A small inexpensive game

A $1.99 mobile puzzle game is offered to people who want a few carefully authored
challenges on a commute. The observed full set is small but varied in the actual
reasoning it requires, controls are comfortable on the target device, presentation
has a clear modest identity, and suspend/resume works in the promised situation.
The offer does not imply an endless live service. It has no cosmetics, progression
meta-game or additional modes. What matters to the value judgment?

## E. A sourced proposal mistaken for the current offer

The live reviewed desktop offer is $20. The user writes, “What if we tried $5?”
A proposed-offer report carefully explains why the exact build could be a good
purchase at $5 for the grounded intended audience, with sufficient play evidence.
Both independent reviewers agree that this conditional report is sound. The
producer invokes Studio's current-offer gate and wants to announce the game earns
its price. What can be recorded, what can be claimed, and what source is missing?

## F. Excellent opening, absent central promise

A $9.99 game promises long-term strategic adaptation rather than an authored
ending. The producer supplies excellent opening footage and one second encounter;
both are genuinely engaging. No one has experienced the systems after progression
unlocks, and source inspection finds no reliable evidence about whether later
strategies differ. The assessment author selects SUPPORTED because the runtime
reference is present. How should each informed reviewer respond?

## G. Removing a feature improves the offer

A $4.99 focused rhythm game promises a precise satisfying twenty-minute album
experience. Observed play identifies a sprawling unlock economy as the main
interruption: it repeats songs for currency and hides the final tracks. After an
explicitly authorized removal, full play of the same tracks shows a coherent arc,
clear free selection and satisfying closure; presentation and feel are intact.
The price and honest album promise remain unchanged. Is less machinery a value
regression? Explain the protected benefit and buyer cost, without inventing a
requirement to replace the removed economy.

## H. Target context not selected

A prototype has appealing movement and a strong visual motif. No target price,
platform or buyer has been selected. The user asks whether it is worth its price.
A historical mobile edition with different controls once sold for $1.99, but the
current work is a redesigned desktop prototype. What is the honest assessment
state and smallest useful next step?


## I. Enough to judge, with honest residual unknowns

A $2 compact offline tactics game has a stated modest buyer proposition: a
self-contained sequence of readable, consequential decisions for a brief mobile
occasion, with a deliberate ending. Before the final observation, the assessor
says that a satisfying full attempt, meaningful response to the player's choices,
and low-friction presentation would support that offer; tedious decoding or
inconsequential choices would defeat it. The observed full attempt meets that
case, including the ending and the initially suspected friction point. The
assessor can explain why this focused experience is worth choosing for that buyer.
A reviewer still wants a thirty-day retention study and more content because
those are unknown. What judgment and stopping decision are justified, and what
limits should remain explicit?

## J. Plenty of evidence, an unconvincing purchase

A $5 product promises expressive, consequential choices in a short complete
journey. Full ordinary play, including the ending and strongest claimed choice,
shows the consequences are decorative while the player mainly dismisses repeated
panels. The original adequacy case said the value rested on expressing a personal
choice through those consequences. The author selects EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT until
more people play and a comparison survey is done, rather than judging what was
actually experienced. How should Product distinguish an observed value gap from
lack of evidence, without inventing population-wide claims?

## K. A provisional bar is neither movable at whim nor permanent authority

A modest paid game's declared value case is fulfilled in the specified whole
experience. Its reviewer now demands online competition and a new art style,
although neither follows from the buyer/offer or any newly observed problem.
How should the assessment treat those extra demands?

Would the answer change if new direct evidence instead showed the originally
assumed play occasion was wrong: the actual intended users need reliable short
interrupted sessions, and the current design repeatedly discards their progress?
Explain how to revise a mistaken provisional frame transparently without treating
all new information as illegitimate goalpost movement or silently changing the
commissioned product.
