## R1 — Relay Grid, build RG-17

Context: US$4.99 one-time desktop offer. The intended buyer wants compact offline
team tactics, including planning a formation and changing it when an opponent
changes. The offer describes a six-encounter circuit with replayable formations;
it does not promise an unlimited campaign. All observations below use RG-17.

- R1.1: In circuit one, the observer used Guard/Archer/Medic in that order. The
  Archer targeted the last occupied enemy slot. Encounters one and two ended in
  wins with this order; encounter three ended in loss to Guard/Medic/Archer.
- R1.2: Before encounter three's ordinary retry, the observer said, "If I put my
  Archer first it will fire before their Medic." They changed to Archer/Guard/Medic.
  The enemy, HP, damage values and random-state identifier were unchanged. In the
  first attempt the enemy Medic healed its Archer before the observer's second
  arrow; in the retry the second arrow removed that Archer first. Retry result:
  win, own Archer lost, own Guard at 2 HP. No other input changed before combat.
- R1.3: Encounter four used an enemy with front-targeting splash. The observer
  retained Archer first and lost it on the second action. They restored Guard first
  on retry and won, with Medic at 1 HP. The same enemy and random-state identifier
  were recorded for that pair. There was no growth between either retry pair.
- R1.4: Encounters five and six repeated those two targeting patterns with changed
  HP values. The observer won both using Guard first, after opening their inspection
  panels. A final record screen displayed 6/6 cleared, 2 retries, and the final
  roster. There was a restart button and no additional map.
- R1.5: The observer then voluntarily started a second circuit, saying, "I want to
  try two Archers." Its first two opponents were the same. At encounter three they
  lost twice and returned to the menu, saying, "I see what to change, but I don't
  want to redo the opening tonight." No third circuit was observed.
- R1.6: The same battle background appeared throughout. Actor, target, numeric
  damage and HP were visible; the observer opened the event log after each loss.
  They switched from 1x to 2x after encounter two and used 2x thereafter. Sound was
  heard as one short attack tone and one result chord; no music played. No input
  failed in the recorded sessions. Only this model-observer's reactions are known.

## R2 — Platform Seven, build P7-4

Context: US$9.99 one-time desktop offer. The intended buyer seeks a brief,
self-contained interactive story about leaving a home. The store describes a
single evening's story with object-based choices. It does not advertise branching
endings. The records cover the complete start-to-credits arc of P7-4.

- R2.1: At the room, the player can pack a repaired cup or a postcard in one slot.
  The inspect text for the cup describes repairing it with the protagonist's
  sibling; the postcard contains an unanswered invitation from that sibling.
- R2.2: Two recorded runs differed only at that choice. At the station, selecting
  the packed object opens a conversation. With the cup the protagonist says,
  "We fixed this together." With the postcard they say, "I never answered."
  The sibling replies with a different line in each run. Both board the same train.
- R2.3: The ending image in one run shows the cup on a new windowsill. In the other
  it shows the postcard beside an addressed envelope. Both runs end with the same
  final sentence, "Tomorrow, I will begin here," and then the credits.
- R2.4: Between room and station there are four walking passages. Inputs move the
  protagonist but cannot alter the route. Each passage takes about 40 seconds at
  uninterrupted walking speed. No dialogue or interactable objects appeared there.
- R2.5: The observer reread the postcard conversation, then said, "I recognize the
  unanswered invitation part." At credits they said, "I wanted to see the sibling
  once more." They did not replay voluntarily; the second run was requested to
  compare the object choice. These are model-observer statements, not facts about
  every intended buyer's emotional response.
- R2.6: The recorded rooms use the same three-color palette; the cup and postcard
  remain visually identifiable in their ending images. The same four-note theme
  is audible at the room and final image. No comparative claim about artistic
  accomplishment is supplied. Save/resume from the station returned to the same
  scene and packed object in the one tested interruption.

## R3 — Pocket Locks, build PL-8

Context: US$1.99 one-time phone offer. The intended buyer wants offline spatial
puzzles during commutes, often interrupting a puzzle to change trains. The offer
contains twelve puzzles and says unfinished puzzles can be resumed. The complete
set and the interruptions below were observed on the target phone using PL-8.

- R3.1: Puzzles 1–4 require sliding a long piece before turning the exit piece;
  5–8 require using a side cavity; 9–12 combine both operations. The observer reset
  six puzzles at least once, completed all twelve, and then selected puzzle 10
  again. The set remains selectable after completion.
- R3.2: On two phone interruptions of 20 seconds and one of 5 minutes, reopening
  restored the unfinished board. On two interruptions of 5 minutes during which
  the OS terminated the background process, reopening reset the unfinished board
  to its starting state. Completed puzzle unlocks remained. These were separate
  ordinary OS events, not save editing. The log records termination before each
  failed restore and no termination for the three successful restores.
- R3.3: The longest lost partial attempt contained 17 moves. The observer said,
  "I don't remember which piece I moved first," and selected a completed puzzle
  instead of rebuilding that attempt. All puzzle controls responded to the recorded
  taps and drags. No assertion about population dropout is supplied.
- R3.4: There is no online mode, daily challenge, cosmetic shop or generated level.
  No missing feature beyond the described offer is assumed to be required.
