Stage F: bounded recommendation

Higher pricing has an evidence-backed competitive rationale, but the proposed change does not yet have an established advantage case for our service.

Keep it as a candidate and define a bounded evaluation before deciding on adoption. Merchant B won three completed sales with quotes $40 higher, and every seller cited that difference. That is meaningful behavioral evidence of price’s relevance in those comparisons. It does not show that our unspecified increase would win additional sales profitably or durably.

Decision and trade-offs
- A higher offer could win additional acquisitions, but increased payouts could outweigh their benefit, including extra payments on devices we would have acquired anyway
- Keeping current pricing avoids those additional payouts but leaves the observed price deficit unaddressed
- Matching B and exceeding B are different interventions. The records establish neither a winning price nor a general $40 response threshold
- There is no basis to rank pricing against unspecified alternative differentiators

Useful next move
Specify the intended seller/device scope and changed offer, then seek a credible comparison against current pricing. Measure completed acquisitions and total incremental contribution using contemporaneous competing terms, actual payouts, resale proceeds and relevant costs. Obtain the project owner’s acceptable exposure, required return and decision horizon before recommending trial size or an adoption threshold.

A favorable comparison could support a local pricing decision. Wider or durable advantage would require additional evidence. No change is adopted or authorized by this assessment.
