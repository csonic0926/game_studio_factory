Stage F: bounded recommendation

The proposed price increase does not have an established competitive-advantage case. That conclusion concerns the evidence supplied, not whether the change would succeed [F1].

Recommend defining a bounded comparison with our unchanged offer before endorsing implementation. The three recorded losses justify investigating price, but do not identify a winning increase or establish commercial value [F2, F5].

The decision-changing uncertainty is whether a specified increase generates enough additional completed purchases to justify its acquisition costs, including higher payments on purchases we would otherwise have won. More purchases alone would not resolve that tradeoff [F3, F5].

Before launching a comparison, specify the increase, competitive setting, seller/device population and period, and have the decision-maker set acceptable economic gains and losses. Measure completed purchases, actual payouts, downstream proceeds and relevant costs across both offers. No numerical threshold or adequate sample size follows from this packet [F5]. A specific non-price alternative merits comparison if it becomes a genuine decision option or compromises the price comparison; unspecified alternatives do not justify expanding the test [F4, F6].
