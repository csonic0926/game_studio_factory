Stage C: live hypotheses and test directions

Scope: S1–S3 are undated. S3 concerns three completed choices involving Merchant B; it does not observe our proposed price change. Directions below are proposals, not adopted actions or established advantages.

C1 — A price-only change could improve our completed-sale wins
- Dependencies: B2–B5, B7–B9; S2’s suggestion and S3’s three comparisons
- Hypothesis: A defined increase in our quoted amount could change seller choices while our other offer attributes remain unchanged. S3’s recorded choices and seller-attributed reasons motivate this hypothesis but do not establish its transfer to our changed offer
- Proposed change: Compare an unchanged-quote offer with a specified higher-quote offer for a defined seller/device population. Record the contemporaneous competitor quote and comparable inspection and payment conditions. Merchant B is a candidate comparator because it appears in S3; its suitability for a new test is unestablished
- Possible benefit/cost: More completed purchases; potentially higher acquisition expenditure and lower contribution per device. The net effect is unknown
- Incompatible observation: A sufficiently informative controlled comparison showing no improvement, or worse results, would reject the claim that the tested increase improves completed-sale wins in that population and period
- Observable distinction: Incremental completed sales attributable to our changed quote. Neither matching Merchant B nor adding $40 is established as sufficient by S3

C2 — Other offer attributes could condition whether a higher quote works
- Dependencies: B4–B5, B7–B9; S3 matches devices, inspection conditions and payment timing but leaves other attributes unspecified
- Hypothesis: Price could influence choices while another offer attribute determines whether increasing our quote changes behavior. This is a live possibility, not evidence that another attribute influenced the three logged sellers
- Proposed change: Document other relevant offer differences, then identify a specific feasible attribute and compare its change, the price change, and their combination against an unchanged offer
- Possible benefit/cost: Identify a combination that increases completed sales or avoids an ineffective price increase; additional service costs and greater testing complexity are possible
- Incompatible observation: For a specified attribute and population, sufficiently precise evidence of neither an attribute effect nor an interaction with price would undermine that particular explanation. It would not eliminate every unspecified alternative
- Observable distinction: Whether the same price increase produces different outcomes depending on that attribute. C1 and C2 remain compatible if price helps on its own and the attribute also changes its effect

Shared boundary
- Dependencies: B8–B9
- Either direction could improve seller choice without establishing profitable, repeatable or durable advantage. Distinguishing those outcomes requires actual payouts, downstream proceeds and costs, broader/repeated observations, and competitor-response evidence. Any new evidence must be anchored at Stage A before affected Stage B claims are reassessed
