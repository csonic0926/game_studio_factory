Stage F — bounded recommendation

Do not treat the higher-price proposal as an established competitive advantage or use this packet alone to justify rollout. Keep it as a candidate for evaluation; the records justify neither adopting nor rejecting its substantive merits [F1, V1].

The possible upside is additional or more valuable buybacks. The trade-off is additional payouts, including on transactions that would have occurred at the comparison offer, and evaluation costs. More volume could still be economically unattractive. Deferring the increase could forgo a genuine benefit, but the packet provides no basis for estimating either outcome [H1–H2].

The useful next move is to specify the comparison offer, proposed increase, device conditions, scope, period and measurable competitive outcome. Separately choose the economic requirement and assess the cost of obtaining informative evidence. Then decide whether a bounded comparison is worth conducting. This recommends preparing an evaluation, not committing to an uncosted test [V2].

Decision-changing uncertainty: Does the specified increase cause a meaningful competitive improvement, and are the gains worth its incremental costs under the chosen criterion? A credible comparison measuring both could support or reject that increase within its tested scope. Uncertain results would leave the question open; a short comparison would not establish durable, market-wide advantage [H1–H2, V2].

The relevant alternative is the defined comparison offer. No existing price or superior alternative strategy is established. Implementation also requires verified authorization; S1’s non-adoption statement and S2’s suggestion do not resolve current authority [F2–F3].
