# First-pass bounded product-value assessments

Date: 2026-10-09. Scope: independent application of the current guidance to the three fictional raw cases in `product/tests/AMBIGUOUS_EXPERIENCE_PACKET.md`. These are judgments of stipulated observations, not firsthand play, real storefront verification, human acceptance, or demand forecasts. The packet's offers and buyers are held fixed. The verdicts below are conditional on those fictional contexts, not current-offer certifications. Case identifiers are exact source locations within that packet.

Only the five authorized files were read: the workflow, Factory skill, reviewer skill, report template, and raw packet. No prior reports, protocol, implementation rationale, or earlier judgments were consulted. The following preserves this exercise's first authored assessment; the provisional bars are stated separately from the evidence application, without claiming they were preregistered before reading the packet.

## R1 — Relay Grid RG-17: SUPPORTED, with bounded moderate confidence

**Provisional purchase case.** At US$4.99, this buyer can reasonably choose a compact six-encounter tactics circuit rather than skip the purchase if inspecting opponents and rearranging a formation produces understandable, consequential adaptations, and trying another formation offers some additional tactical discovery. A large campaign, luxurious presentation, or indefinite return habit is unnecessary. Merely watching predetermined wins, or replay consisting entirely of unchanged solved openings without a worthwhile tactical alternative, would defeat this case. The finite circuit must have positive tactical value; its low price alone is no defense.

**Competing explanations and the causal distinction.** One explanation is that formation planning changes battle timing and protection in ways the player can learn and exploit. The strongest opposing account is narrower than “nothing ever changes”: an initially engaging positional trick could soon reduce to a small solved recipe, with repetition costing more than the remaining discoveries are worth. Wins, losses, two retries, a clear record screen, and an initial voluntary return are compatible with either account. None alone proves replay value.

The factual question about whether rearrangement actually matters is settled by R1.2–R1.3. A timing-sensitive account predicts that moving the Archer forward can remove the enemy Archer before its Medic's healing protects it; an account in which formation has no combat consequence cannot explain that order-specific sequence with enemy, numerical values, random-state identifier, and all other pre-combat inputs held constant. R1.2 records that exact reversal, with a costly rather than effortless win. R1.3 then defeats the broader claim that “Archer first” is a universally dominant discovery: against front-targeting splash it dies early, whereas restoring Guard first produces a win under the matched enemy and random-state condition, without growth. These are two local causal demonstrations, not proof that every formation or later encounter requires equally rich decisions.

R1.4 is real counterevidence to lasting depth: the final two encounters recycle the two targeting patterns with changed HP, and Guard first wins both. Opening inspection panels does not establish that those inspections caused either win or that HP changes added a new decision. R1.5 supports both initial attraction to a different roster and a concrete replay cost: a two-Archer attempt reaches a problem the observer thinks they can address, but repeating the opening outweighs their interest that evening. It does not show that the proposed correction works, that replay becomes deeply varied, or that the player will return later.

**Buyer-value judgment.** The positive purchase support is the experienced cycle of reading a threat, making a justified formation change, and seeing timing/protection turn a loss into a precarious win, within a circuit that reaches a legible conclusion. For the specified compact-tactics buyer, that small but complete tactical arc and the exposed alternative-roster problem warrant this modest offer, even though replay is limited and frictionful. The protected benefit of a bounded circuit is a compact, finishable tactical exercise; the cost is redoing solved material to reach an experiment's point of interest. R1.5 makes that cost substantial, so this is not a recommendation for a durable mastery game.

The repeated background and minimal audio in R1.6 do not themselves prove good or bad aesthetics. Their observed role is limited: actor/target/damage/HP visibility, usable logs after losses, and working 2x playback support deciphering and iterating on the tactical changes. Switching to 2x is not evidence by itself that 1x is intolerable. Sparse presentation does not supply the purchase value, but it does not obstruct the demonstrated value here.

**Sufficiency, limits, and stop.** The completed circuit plus a voluntary alternate-roster attempt reaches the modest replay claim enough for this bounded judgment; it does not establish endless discovery, balanced rosters, or population willingness to pay. Those stronger claims are not needed for this recommendation. Confidence remains moderate because the second circuit ends before the new formation problem is solved. Product should stop expanding the assessment and retain the opening-repetition objection. If a meaningful changed build claims better replay, Product should inspect whether an alternate-roster correction produces new tactical learning without imposing excessive solved repetition. No added campaign, art production, or mandatory third run is warranted merely to certify this case.

## R2 — Platform Seven P7-4: SUPPORTED, with modest confidence in the value weighting

**Provisional purchase case.** For this US$9.99 brief, self-contained story buyer, a worthwhile reason to buy is to enact a personally legible way of carrying a relationship out of an old home, with the selected object developed through conversation into a concluding image. The authored arc needs a satisfying emotional or interpretive payoff; having functional object buttons is insufficient. A choice whose meaning disappears, or a conclusion that leaves the promised leaving-home arc unfulfilled, would defeat the recommendation. The alternative of simply declining a short story purchase remains reasonable; neither short length nor formal promise compliance automatically earns this price.

**Competing explanations.** The favorable account is expressive agency: choosing the cup or postcard changes how the departure is understood, even though it does not change the train. The strongest objection is that these are thin variations within a predetermined departure, separated by empty traversal, so the buyer receives too little developed interaction and relational payoff for the offer. Both accounts predict the same train, common final sentence, four fixed walking passages, and no voluntary replay. The latter observation is especially weak as a discriminator because replay is not the buying reason and the second run was requested.

R2.1–R2.3 distinguish a factual claim of no consequential expression from a shared destination with different expressive consequences. If the choice were forgotten or merely replaced an inventory icon, the station conversation and concluding composition would not carry the object's established relationship meaning. Instead, the repaired-with-sibling cup leads to an explicit shared-memory statement and appears on the new windowsill; the unanswered-invitation postcard leads to an admission of nonresponse and appears beside an addressed envelope. The sibling's reply differs too. These observations support a causal chain from choice to conversational stance to retained final motif. They do not establish branching outcomes, changed later options, a reconciled relationship, or that the envelope was sent to the sibling. A sophisticated “too thin” criticism can acknowledge every one of these facts; the remaining disagreement is about their value, not missing causal evidence.

**Buyer-value judgment.** The affirmative case is that the choice gives the player a small authorship role in the departure: emphasize a repaired shared past, or acknowledge an unanswered connection, then see that chosen object accompany the new beginning. The repeated four-note theme and identifiable object in the ending give the final image a concrete connection to the room rather than leaving it an unrelated train ending (R2.6). This is my interpretation of the observed composition, not evidence that the music universally causes emotion or that the art is exceptional.

For the stated buyer of a brief interactive leaving-home story, that complete thematic through-line is a defensible reason to choose this purchase at US$9.99. The protected benefit of the fixed destination is a focused authored departure in which the object changes its personal meaning; its cost is narrow mechanical agency and no opportunity to pursue a different relationship outcome. R2.4's roughly 160 seconds of noninteractive walking also count against the experience: there is no evidence of new dialogue, decisions, or particular aesthetic payoff in those intervals, and I do not invent one. I weigh that dead time and limited variation below the personally interpretable object-to-conversation-to-ending arc, rather than claiming they disappear because branching was never promised.

R2.5 provides a limited positive signal of recognition and a genuine adverse signal of wanting another sibling encounter. It does not decide the tradeoff. The desired reunion could be rewarding expansion or evidence of insufficient closure for that observer; it is not proof that the leaving-home arc fails. My bounded recommendation rests on the departure's completed transformation and persistent relationship motif, not on dismissing the observer's disappointment. A buyer for whom direct relational resolution is essential could reasonably decline, but I have not silently replaced the specified buyer with that narrower audience.

**Sufficiency, limits, and stop.** The complete arc, controlled object comparison, actual visual/audio observations, and tested station interruption cover the value-bearing claim. The factual consequences are known; the residual disagreement is a subjective weighting of expressive payoff against thin interaction and walking. Another run would repeat those facts rather than settle taste, and no population study is required. Product should stop here with the modest-confidence recommendation and its material drawbacks visible. Evidence that these stipulated object consequences were absent in the actual build, or new audience authority making direct sibling resolution essential, would reopen the assessment. This does not authorize new scenes, a changed price, or a claim of demonstrated customer satisfaction.

## R3 — Pocket Locks PL-8: VALUE_GAP, with high confidence in the stated-context shortcoming

**Provisional purchase case.** At US$1.99, twelve offline spatial puzzles can be a complete worthwhile commute purchase if the two spatial operations develop into combinations and an interrupted attempt survives ordinary changes of train. The buyer does not need a daily challenge, online mode, store, or generated content. Losing meaningful unfinished work during the intended use would defeat the offer despite competent puzzles and a low price.

**Decisive evidence.** R3.1 supplies genuine positive puzzle evidence: the set develops two operations into combined problems, the observer needs several resets, completes it, and chooses puzzle 10 again. Resets do not prove quality in isolation, but here the observable progression and chosen return support some repeat puzzle interest. R3.2–R3.3 nonetheless establish the central disappointment: both observed ordinary OS terminations erase unfinished boards, one losing 17 moves, after which the observer cannot reconstruct the attempt and chooses an already completed puzzle instead.

The successful short and five-minute interruptions are compatible both with durable resume and with state surviving only while the process survives. They cannot establish the promised durability. The two terminated-process events distinguish those behaviors: durable resume should restore the partial board after reopening; PL-8 instead returns its starting state while retaining completed unlocks. The nonterminated five-minute success also means elapsed five-minute duration alone does not explain the observed pattern. This establishes an observed failure condition, not a code-level diagnosis or a population failure probability.

**Buyer-value judgment.** For this same frequent-interruption commute buyer, preserving a developing spatial solution outweighs the inexpensive price and positive finite puzzle progression. Persistent unlocks protect completed progress but do not protect the unfinished reasoning the offer specifically says can be resumed. The small offline set coherently forgoes live services and endless novelty, but it cannot trade away reliable interruption recovery without undermining its promised use. There is no need to manufacture a second whole-product argument: this observed central failure is enough for a gap.

**Sufficiency, limits, and next action.** Evidence is sufficient to reject this build's specified offer; unseen additional puzzle depth or market studies would not repair the encountered failure. Product should route the bounded resume defect to the authorized implementation owner. After an authorized fix, test an unfinished board across ordinary OS process termination and verify the exact partial board restores; also retain checks for nonterminated interruption and completed unlocks. That changed experience would remove this decisive objection and permit reassessment against the already observed puzzle value. No precise failure rate, root cause, human dropout rate, or universal phone compatibility is asserted, and none is required to make the present gap judgment.

## Disposition

R1 and R2 are bounded positive model judgments with explicit costs, not proof of buyer consensus. R3 is a completed negative assessment with a targeted changed-build verification condition. No Factory files or fictional observations were changed; no acceptance, independent review gate, current-offer certification, or production action is claimed.
